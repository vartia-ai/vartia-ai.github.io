06 · Methodology

How a finding earns that name

The workflow favors traceable proof over unsupported certainty. Upstream zlib is not normalized into the baseline tables yet, so this assessment combines raw CPE-linked CVEs, fix-reference verification in the official repo, five CVE-specific CodeQL pre/fix/full-codebase backtests, local historical replay, fuzz-generated witness capture, and current-revision build/test evidence.

Raw historical corpus extraction

Collected 11 upstream zlib CVEs from raw cve/cve_cpe tables keyed by cpe:2.3:a:zlib:zlib:* in the local SQLite database.

Fix-reference verification

Verified that five upstream GitHub fix commits referenced by the local DB are present in the official madler/zlib develop history.

Case-specific CodeQL validation

Authored and backtested five CVE-specific queries for CVE-2022-37434 and CVE-2016-9840/9841/9842/9843; each rule returns 1 result on the vulnerable revision, 0 on the fix, and 0 additional results across the full current HEAD codebase.

Historical witness replay and fuzzing

Built vulnerable and fixed worktrees for all five commit-backed cases, replayed each one with a deterministic harness or sanitizer witness, and ran bounded fuzzing campaigns that retain or generate vulnerable witness artifacts while the fixed builds complete cleanly.

Current revision validation

Built the official HEAD and ran the stock make test suite successfully to establish a minimal current-scope baseline.

Evidence Standard
No current issue is surfaced without executable proof. Historical cases are promoted only when the same witness reproduces on the vulnerable revision, disappears or runs clean on the fixed revision, and the CVE-specific CodeQL rule does not trigger on the fixed or full current codebase.

08 · Targeted Remediation Plan

What to fix first and how to verify closure

The first engineering pass should keep closing classes in the inflate family before chasing diffuse hygiene work. The historical validation now shows that bounds, signedness, and pointer-bias invariants are the highest-leverage targets across supported code.

Inflate-family bounds normalization

Targetinflate.c, inffast.c, inftrees.c, inftrees.h
ActionCentralize bounds checks and reject malformed headers or tables before pointer rebasing, state shifts, or table writes.
BenefitCovers the dominant hotspot that appears in 8 of 11 upstream historical records, 5 of 7 HIGH/CRITICAL cases, and 4 of the 5 locally replayed cases.
VerifyKeep the five validated CodeQL rules and harnesses as regression fixtures, then extend the same guardrail pattern to unreplayed raw records if they remain in scope.

Checked arithmetic and signedness hardening

Targetinflate state math, crc32 big-endian code, MiniZip length calculations
ActionReplace implicit signed and width assumptions with checked arithmetic, explicit range guards, and preconditioned pointer movement.
BenefitTargets the 50% integer-overflow share in the mapped corpus and reduces repeat numeric-to-bounds failure modes.
VerifyAdd UBSan and pointer-bias regression coverage around the exact historical shift and pre-decrement patterns proved in the 2016 and 2022 cases.

Contrib/minizip scope boundary

Targetcontrib/minizip/zip.c and release/security documentation
ActionEither vendor and test MiniZip as supported code or document and isolate it as out-of-scope contrib code.
BenefitRemoves ambiguity around the recent CRITICAL MiniZip record and keeps the supported security surface explicit.
VerifyTie the policy to release gates and either add tests or remove the component from supported builds.
